Significance is a verdict: the evidence is now strong enough that "it was just luck" stops being a reasonable defense. That evidence comes from exactly two places, how big the effect is and how many users you have. Move both and watch the verdict change.

Explained in non-technical terms
A whisper can be significant in a quiet room with many listeners. A shout can fail in a storm with only three. That's why a tiny effect needs a big audience to convict and a big effect can win on a small one: the verdict weighs the size of the difference against the number of witnesses.

At AmpliStore a lipstick recommender shows a +0.4 point lift on 4,000 shoppers and reads 78%, which isn't a verdict, it's an unfinished trial, so you keep running. Three weeks later the same lift on 80,000 shoppers reads 98%. Meanwhile a gift-wrap upsell jumps 12% and clears the bar in three days on 6,000 shoppers. Same bar, two roads. Two traps sit under it: statistically significant isn't the same as worth shipping, so read the confidence interval edges, and not significant doesn't mean no effect, it often just means not enough data yet. And 94.9% is not "basically 95," the bar exists precisely so hope can't move it.
