Nobody stores which variant each of two million shoppers got. There's no lookup table. The shopper's ID and the flag's salt go through a hash that lands on a fixed number, and that number decides the variant the same way every time, on any device.

Sort them once, then again. The IDs are the same, so the results are too.
Explained in non-technical terms
It's a sorting hat that never changes its mind. It reads the name, does its secret math, and assigns a house. Same name, same house, every time, on the phone, the laptop, or after a reinstall. There's no notebook of past decisions, the math itself is the memory.

At AmpliStore a shopper opens the shade-finder test on her phone at lunch, her laptop at night, and after reinstalling next week. The hash of her ID plus this flag's salt always lands on 37, so she always gets treatment. One gotcha: an anonymous shopper bucketed by device can jump variants once she logs in and the identities merge, and for B2B you bucket by company ID so everyone in an org sees the same thing.
