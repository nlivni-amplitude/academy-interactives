The salt is the ingredient in the bucketing hash that decides the shuffle. Keep it and the sorting never moves. Change it and every user gets re-sorted, which is exactly what you want for a fresh run and a disaster mid-experiment.

Explained in non-technical terms
Change the salt and the whole school gets re-sorted, everyone could land in a different house. That's great when you want a clean fresh shuffle, and catastrophic if it happens partway through a live test.

At AmpliStore two weeks into the checkout test, an engineer edits the salt to clean things up. Thousands of shoppers silently swap variants, people mid-purchase see the other checkout, and the data mixes both experiences into something unreadable. The right use is a deliberate new run after fixing broken instrumentation, with the previous data excluded, which is why the platform gates salt changes behind approvals.
