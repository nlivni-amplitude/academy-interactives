Explained in non-technical terms
It's a dashcam. It's always quietly remembering the last 30 seconds, and it only saves the footage when something interesting happens, the trigger. So when the crash happens, you also get the 30 seconds leading up to it, the context that explains what the driver was reacting to.

The buffer means you see the last 30 seconds before assignment plus everything after, so you watch what the shopper hit right before the moment you care about. Triggers can fire on an individual gate exposure, a specific experiment variant, all exposures at once, or any specific event. And the recording is focused: you're not drowning in the whole session library, just the treatment sessions that dropped at the step you're investigating.
