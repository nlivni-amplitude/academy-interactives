You want iOS shoppers or US shoppers to see a feature. You write one rule with both conditions, and an iOS shopper in Singapore never gets it. The rule did exactly what it said, which wasn't what you meant.

One rule, two conditions
Rule 1:
platform = iOS
country = US
Conditions stacked inside a single rule are always joined with AND. Only shoppers who are both on iOS and in the US pass. That iOS shopper in Singapore fails.
Two separate rules
Rule 1: platform = iOS
Rule 2: country = US
Split across two rules, fall-through does the work: a shopper who misses Rule 1 still gets checked against Rule 2. iOS or US shoppers pass. That's your OR.
Explained in non-technical terms
One rule with two conditions is a bouncer holding a single checklist: you have to match every line on it to get in. Two separate rules are two different doors down the hall: clear the first or the second and you're through. Piling conditions onto one checklist narrows who gets in. Adding another door widens it.
