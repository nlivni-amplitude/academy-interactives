Your experiment shows treatment ahead by 4 percentage points. Before believing the feature did that, it's worth asking how often pure randomness alone produces a gap that size, when nothing actually changed.

100 simulated experiments. Both arms get the identical 20% true conversion rate, no real difference between them. Each square is one simulated run.
Press run to see how often luck alone clears a 4-point gap.
A p-value is that fraction. It's not the odds the feature works, it's the odds that noise alone, with zero real effect, would still hand you a gap this big or bigger. A small p-value means that fraction is small, so the gap you saw is unusual enough to take seriously.

Run it again and the count moves a little, that's expected, it's still random. What should stay roughly stable is that a 4-point gap isn't rare enough to ignore, but it isn't so rare that seeing it once proves anything either.
