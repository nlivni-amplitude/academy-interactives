Day 3 shows a gap so you call it and ship. Day 3 shows a gap the other way, and you'd have called that too. Both instincts have the same problem: neither day was when you planned to look.

One 14-day experiment, no real difference between arms, tracked day by day. The line is the measured gap each day, it wanders because it's just noise.
Press run to see how much a "nothing changed" gap moves day to day.
The line crosses "looks significant" more than once along the way, in both directions, purely from noise settling differently each day. Stopping the moment it crosses that line, in whichever direction favors the story you want, is what peeking costs you. The fix isn't to stop checking, it's to only make the ship decision on the day you committed to before the experiment started.

A tool that runs the same math sequentially, correcting for every look instead of just the last one, is built for teams that want to check in without paying this cost. Left unadjusted, checking daily and stopping the first time it looks good is close to guaranteed to eventually look good, whether or not anything is actually different.
