A hypothesis you write before an experiment is what stops you from rationalizing whatever result you see afterward. A complete one has three parts, and assembling one below shows what happens when a part is missing.

Explained in non-technical terms
It's calling your shot before the coin lands. If you say out loud "heads, and here's why" before the flip, you can't quietly claim you meant tails afterward. Writing the action, the number, and the reason down first is how you keep from rewriting the story to match whatever the scorecard shows.

All three parts are load-bearing. Skip the action and there's nothing to run. Skip the predicted outcome and any result counts as a win, since you never said what you expected. Skip the rationale and you can't tell a real effect from a fluke, because you had no reason it should work. AmpliStore writes all three before launch so the scorecard settles the question instead of becoming a Rorschach test.
