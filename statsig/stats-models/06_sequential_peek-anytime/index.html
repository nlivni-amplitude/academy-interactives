A fixed-horizon test is a cake you can't open the oven on: peek early and the false-positive math is ruined. Sequential testing puts a glass door on the oven so you can look as often as you want. The catch is the glass starts foggy.

The shaded band is how obvious a result has to be to call it on that day. Watch it tighten as data comes in.
Note
Early on the boundary is wide, so only a huge, obvious effect clears it. As users accumulate the band narrows and smaller true effects become callable. That widening early boundary is the price of being allowed to peek: it keeps the false-positive rate honest no matter how many times you look.

At AmpliStore this ships a one-page checkout the week before a mega sale. The team peeks daily with a clear conscience, and on day 6 the line clears the band, so they call the win early and lock it in before the sale. Had checkout tanked, the same math would have flagged the regression by day 2 or 3 instead of week 4. This is Amplitude's default engine. Statsig offers it as an opt-in overlay, positioned mainly for catching regressions early rather than shipping early.
