The growth PM ships one homepage test at AmpliStore but attaches twelve metrics to it: revenue, average order value, quiz starts, wishlist adds, reviews read. At the usual bar, there's roughly a 46% chance at least one of them looks like a win purely by luck.

Only one of these twelve metrics reflects a real effect. Run it both ways and watch what "wins."
Make one wish and it comes true, that's magic. Make twelve wishes and one comes true, that's just odds. Checking twelve metrics is making twelve wishes: one tends to clear the bar by chance alone. Bonferroni's rule is that the more metrics you check, the stronger the proof each one has to show before you believe it.

Without correction the fake "wishlist adds +9%" lights up alongside the one real effect, and you can't tell them apart. Bonferroni divides the bar across the twelve, roughly 0.004 each, and the luck-driven win disappears. Statsig offers it as an option plus Benjamini-Hochberg as a less strict alternative Amplitude doesn't have. Amplitude applies it automatically for multi-metric tests and turns it off under Bayesian, which controls false positives its own way.
