The CMO won't read p-values. For a loyalty-tier renaming test at AmpliStore, a Bayesian read answers in the language they actually want: the probability the new names lift repeat purchase, and what it costs if you're wrong.

Starting belief: a vague guess centered on zero lift. Each batch of data sharpens it.
Explained in non-technical terms
Guess how many candies are in the jar. Someone shows you a handful and you adjust. Another handful, you adjust again. You start unsure and end confident. That's the Bayesian move: every batch of data updates the current best guess rather than starting over. And it reports like a weather forecast, "92% chance the new version is better," instead of a p-value.

The team reports 92% probability the new names lift repeat purchase, with an expected downside of 0.1% if they're wrong, and the decision gets made in one meeting. They also encode a prior from last year's rebrand test, so this one starts smart instead of from scratch. Statsig exposes Bayesian as a selectable type that supports those priors.
