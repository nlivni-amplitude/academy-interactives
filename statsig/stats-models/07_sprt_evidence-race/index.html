Picture a tug-of-war between "it works" and "it doesn't." Every new shopper's data pulls the rope a little one way or the other. The instant the rope crosses a line, the test is over. A strong effect wins in seconds; two even teams keep pulling.

Pick a scenario. The line is the accumulated evidence, the two edges are the decision boundaries.
This is what SPRT does: it tracks the ratio of evidence for "there's an effect" against "there isn't," and it stops the moment that ratio crosses a boundary in either direction. Obvious winners cross fast, obvious duds cross the other way fast, and genuinely close calls keep collecting data because the evidence hasn't tipped yet.

At AmpliStore an "only 3 left" urgency badge has a big, immediate effect, so under SPRT the evidence smashes through the boundary in 5 days on a test scoped for 3 weeks. That's 16 days of conversion banked and the test slot freed. Statsig exposes SPRT as a selectable analytics type. Amplitude doesn't call it out as a mode because its default sequential engine already is mSPRT, the same family, so the answer to "does Amplitude have SPRT" is yes, it's the engine underneath.
