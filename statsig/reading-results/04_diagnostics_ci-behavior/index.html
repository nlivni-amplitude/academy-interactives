Guessing candies in a jar: after two peeks your guess is "somewhere between 50 and 500," useless. After a hundred peeks it's "between 180 and 220," useful. A p-value above 0.05 doesn't mean no candy, it means you haven't looked enough yet. The interval shrinks as the data accumulates, and the point estimate was always sitting near the truth.

This is also why some intervals stay stubbornly wide even with a clean split: heavy-tailed metrics, where most users spend a little and one whale spends a lot, carry huge variance. When one metric row won't tighten, check whether variance reduction is already applied, run it longer, or read a higher-frequency proxy metric instead of waiting on the noisy one.
