The scorecard is clean, no sample ratio mismatch, participation looks sane. Now comes the actual decision. Each scenario below has a defensible call and some tempting wrong ones. Pick one to see the reasoning.

The synthesis routine, every time: start with the primary, check the secondaries agree with it, then walk every guardrail. A clear primary win with all guardrails within tolerance is a ship. A guardrail regression that's statistically real is an investigation first. A primary interval still crossing zero with planned time left is a keep-running.
Scenario 1. Primary is +3.0%, CI [+0.5%, +5.5%]. The error-rate guardrail is +2.0%, CI [+0.1%, +3.9%]. Both p under 0.05. The PM says the primary is clearly positive, ship it.
Investigate first. Both intervals exclude zero, so the guardrail regression is exactly as real as the win. There are three defensible moves: ship and fix if the cause is known and benign, ship and instrument, or hold. "Ship because the primary is stronger" isn't one of them, a real win and a real regression are both true at once.
Scenario 2. Primary is +12%, CI [-3%, +27%], p = 0.10. You've run 6 of 14 planned days. The manager says it's not significant, kill it.
Keep running. A p-value above 0.05 means not enough data to confirm, not that the effect is zero. The point estimate is large and the planned duration isn't done. Let it reach the horizon you committed to before you started, then make the call.
Scenario 3. Primary is +2.1%, CI [+0.8%, +3.4%]. Every guardrail is flat with intervals hugging zero. You ran the full planned duration and the secondaries are consistent.
Ship. This is the clean case: a real primary win, consistent secondaries, quiet guardrails, and the full duration honored. Re-running forever has a cost too, both the opportunity you're sitting on and the multiple-testing risk of looking again and again. Make the call.
