A 46/54 split isn't "close enough" when the chi-squared test says an imbalance this large has under a 0.01% chance of happening randomly. That means something systematic is filtering one arm differently: a crash before exposure logs, bot traffic, conditional logging, truncated logs. More data makes it worse, not better, because you're adding more biased data on top.

The diagnostic is the SRM p-value over time. Noisy and bouncing around means it might be a false alarm. Trending toward zero means a real bug, and the fix is to find the cause, then restart the experiment clean. A compromised split can't be read past, no matter how good the scorecard underneath it looks.
