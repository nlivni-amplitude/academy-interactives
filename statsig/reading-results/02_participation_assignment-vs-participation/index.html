You invited 20 people to the party. Three showed up and danced. The party wasn't broken, most invitees just didn't come. If the metric is "completed a purchase," most exposed users were never going to buy on that visit. Participation is assignment times how often the event actually fires, and for e-commerce that rate is small: 140,000 exposures against 2,100 purchases is a 1.5% purchase rate, which is normal.

Only participants feed the metric math, so the gap between assigned and participated isn't loss, it's the funnel. One related read: the last day or two of any metric looks artificially flat because those events are still being processed. Report on baked data, not on the trailing edge that's still filling in.
